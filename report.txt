  - "payment help" is ambiguous between reporting a payment problem and asking

  - "contactingassistant" is a one-token, malformed-style message.
  - A request for a human agent being labeled "complaint" is operationally
    important; human-handoff signals should be handled conservatively.

For routing, these cases argue for human review or a clarification prompt
rather than automatic action when confidence is low or the message is short.

10. INTERPRETATION
------------------

What the results suggest:
  - Simple, interpretable TF-IDF models can separate these six synthetic
    customer-support intents almost perfectly in this benchmark.
  - The task is learnable from lexical cues alone; no transformer model is
    required to reproduce this result on this dataset.
  - The realistic operational value is in routing suggestions, not autonomous
    decisions.

What the results do NOT establish:
  - Production accuracy on real customer messages.
  - Performance on Indonesian, WhatsApp, or clinic-specific conversations.
  - Reliable confidence estimates for threshold-based automation.
  - Detection of out-of-scope or multiple-intent messages.

Recommended human-review cases:
  - Requests for a human agent (must not be buried as complaints).
  - Ambiguous payment messages and short messages with little context.
  - Out-of-scope topics such as appointment booking, treatment questions,
    symptom reports, or adverse events.
  - Any low-confidence prediction after proper probability calibration.


11. LIMITATIONS
---------------

1. Domain shift: the dataset is synthetic English customer-support text, not
   real Indonesian aesthetic-clinic messages.
2. Missing classes: booking, treatment/pricing enquiries, symptom reports and
   multi-intent or unknown messages are outside the taxonomy.
3. Safety: the classifier must never auto-reply to clinical symptom or
   adverse-event messages; independent safety and escalation rules are needed.
4. Confidence calibration: the reported maximum class probabilities are not
   calibrated and are shown for exploration only.
5. Template similarity: exact deduplication was performed, but near-duplicate
   synthetic templates can still inflate random-split scores.
6. Tie handling: Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression tie on validation macro-F1
   at four decimals; the selection is deterministic but not a meaningful
   performance difference.

Recommended next step: obtain permitted, de-identified real-world messages,
define a business-specific intent taxonomy, annotate a held-out evaluation set
with quality checks, and evaluate routing with human oversight.
